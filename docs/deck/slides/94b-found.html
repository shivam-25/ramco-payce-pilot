WHAT MEASUREMENT FOUND THAT REVIEW DID NOT

Six defects. Four were found by tests that had just passed.

Not by reading the code, and not by reviewing the design. By a number moving in a direction nobody expected.

The cache ignored time

September was served June’s answer — the founding incident, reintroduced by my own optimisation. Entries now carry a validity window.

The gate understated its own work

Identifiers buried in event histories were masked correctly, then reported as “none found”. Right answer, wrong accounting.

A pointer passed as an answer

“The rate is in KB-0140, see Confluence.” Telling a customer where an answer lives is not answering them.

That is the argument for gates with numbers rather than a review with opinions. The other two were a threshold comparing two different scales and a chunk that named its own replacement. A seventh escaped every gate — the pilot invented product behaviour, and I found it by typing a ticket into the console. Gates catch what you thought to measure. Using the thing catches the rest.
